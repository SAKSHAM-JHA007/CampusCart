CAMPUSCART — PRODUCT REQUIREMENTS DOCUMENT (v2)

Status: Draft for review
Last updated: 27 Sep 2026
Changed from v1: restructured around goals, differentiation, and safety
instead of a flat feature list. "Why this changed" notes flag the
biggest fixes.

================================================================
1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
================================================================

CampusCart is a localized, student-only marketplace for buying and
selling second-hand college and hostel essentials, launching first at
BMSIT Yelahanka. It targets the natural senior-to-junior resale cycle:
outgoing students have items they no longer need, incoming students
need the same items at a lower price than retail.

Why this changed: v1 jumped straight into features. A PRD needs to
open by stating what success looks like and who else is already
trying to solve this — both were missing.

================================================================
2. PROBLEM STATEMENT
================================================================

Students buy books, hostel essentials, and small appliances when they
join college, then have no good way to offload them when they leave or
upgrade. Juniors need the same items but don't want retail prices.
Today this is solved informally through WhatsApp groups, seniors and
juniors networks, and general classifieds (OLX, Facebook Marketplace)
— all of which lack local, trustworthy, search-and-filter discovery
built for a campus context.

Before building further: run a quick survey (30–50 students at BMSIT)
to confirm how often students currently buy/sell used goods, what they
currently use, and what would make them switch. The doc currently
asserts the problem without any data behind it — even a small sample
beats none.

================================================================
3. MARKET & COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE (new)
================================================================

This space is more crowded than v1 acknowledged. Known players and
lookalikes:

- CampusKart — "India's #1 student marketplace": textbooks,
  electronics, lab kits, hostel essentials, verified college students,
  senior-to-junior framing. Name is one letter from CampusCart.
  Positioning is nearly identical.

- Campurchase — claims live presence across 24+ IITs/IIMs/BITS/NITs.
  Hosted as a prototype (Lovable app); traction claims should be
  treated as unverified marketing copy, but the concept overlap is
  real.

- Unicycle, PassItOn (VIT), CampusSwap, CampusMart, IIT Mandi
  Marketplace — independent student-built clones of the same idea.
  Confirms this is a well-trodden concept — differentiation and
  execution will matter more than the idea itself.

- WhatsApp/Telegram groups, OLX, Quikr, Facebook Marketplace — free,
  already have liquidity, zero switching cost. The real incumbent.
  Most students already have a working (if messy) solution.

There is even a literal "Campus Cart" — a different capstone project
for campus food ordering, unrelated field but identical name.

Action items before further investment:
- Run a trademark / app-store-name / domain search. Given "CampusKart"
  already exists with the same positioning, a name change may be worth
  considering.
- Write down, explicitly, why a student would choose CampusCart over
  CampusKart or their existing WhatsApp group. (See Section 6 — this
  needs a real answer, not just "we built it for BMSIT.")

================================================================
4. GOALS & SUCCESS METRICS — PHASE 1 (BMSIT) (new)
================================================================

BMSIT has roughly 6,000 students (about 5,500 undergraduate, 500
postgraduate). Use that as the denominator for realistic targets.
These are starting targets — recalibrate after the first two weeks of
real usage.

  Metric                              | Target (first 3 months)
  -------------------------------------|--------------------------
  Active listings live                 | 150+
  Weekly active buyers                 | 250–300
  Listing -> contact rate              | >25%
  Contact -> completed handoff rate    | >40% (self-reported)
  Sellers who list 2+ items            | 20%
  Reported safety incidents            | 0 (non-negotiable)

Phase 2 (expansion beyond BMSIT) should be gated on hitting these, not
on a calendar date.

================================================================
5. TARGET USERS & PERSONAS
================================================================

- Ananya, final-year student, about to graduate: has a mattress,
  induction stove, and two semesters of textbooks she no longer needs.
  Wants to sell quickly, with minimal hassle, before she vacates her
  hostel.

- Rahul, first-year fresher: just moved into a PG, needs the same
  essentials on a tight budget, doesn't know any seniors yet, and
  currently relies on scrolling a 500-person WhatsApp group hoping to
  catch a relevant message.

================================================================
6. WHY STUDENTS WOULD SWITCH (new — the differentiation gap)
================================================================

- WhatsApp groups: lack search, filters, and persistent listings —
  everything is buried in chat history. CampusCart should own
  structured search/filter and persistent, categorized listings.

- OLX/Quikr/Facebook Marketplace: not student-verified, not hyperlocal
  to campus, no hostel-life context (condition, pickup logistics).
  CampusCart should own a verified-student trust layer and
  campus-level location filtering.

- CampusKart / other clones: genuinely unresolved. This is an open
  question (see Section 17) that needs a concrete answer before more
  building happens.

================================================================
7. CORE USER JOURNEYS (new — replaces vague feature bullets)
================================================================

US-1 — Seller lists an item
As a verified student, I can list an item with photos, price,
condition, and pickup location so buyers nearby can find it.
Acceptance: listing requires 1+ photo, category, price, and condition;
goes live after a moderation check.

US-2 — Buyer discovers and expresses interest
As a buyer, I can search/filter listings by category, price, and
distance, and express interest in one so the seller is notified.
Acceptance: interest triggers a notification to the seller with
buyer's verified name and college; no payment collected by the
platform.

US-3 — Buyer and seller complete a safe handoff
As a buyer or seller, I can view a suggested safe on-campus meetup
point and mark the transaction complete after meeting.
Acceptance: platform never displays exact hostel room/address; both
sides can mark "completed" or "fell through."

US-4 — Report a bad actor
As any user, I can report a listing or a user, and expect a response
within a defined SLA.
Acceptance: report reaches an admin queue; repeat-offender accounts
are auto-flagged for review.

================================================================
8. PRODUCT SCOPE & FEATURES
================================================================

8.1 Authentication & Trust Verification
Sign up / login / logout / forgot password, plus college email
verification — moved into MVP (v1 deferred this; for a platform built
on in-person cash handoffs, unverified accounts are a safety risk from
day one, not a nice-to-have).

8.2 Student Profile
Name, college, hostel/PG, general (not exact) location, contact info,
profile picture, listings, transaction history. Course/branch/year
kept optional — reduces signup friction without losing anything
essential.

8.3 Listings (Add Product)
Product name, category, description, price, condition, photos, pickup
location (general area, not exact address), contact preference. Add a
lightweight prohibited-items policy link (no restricted goods, no
scams, no counterfeit).

8.4 Discovery — Search, Categories, Filters
Kept largely as in v1: search by keyword; categories (Books, Notes,
Electronics, Hostel Essentials, Furniture, Appliances, Bags, Other);
filters by price, category, condition, distance.

8.5 Express Interest & Reserve (renamed from "Cart & Checkout")

Why this changed: v1's cart/checkout metaphor assumes multi-item
inventory and a completed online payment — neither exists here. Most
listings are single unique used items, and the doc itself defers
payment to a later phase. Naming it "cart & checkout" sets an
expectation the product doesn't deliver.

Buyer expresses interest in an item -> seller is notified and confirms
-> both parties get each other's contact info and a suggested safe
meetup point. No payment is processed by the platform in this phase.

8.6 Transaction / Handoff Log (renamed from "Orders")
Buyers and sellers see past interests expressed, confirmed handoffs,
and outcomes. This is a log, not an order-management system, until
real payments exist.

8.7 My Listings (Seller View)
Add, edit, delete, mark as sold/available, update price. Views/
enquiries counter can wait for a later phase (see Section 12).

8.8 Admin Panel
View/verify users, manage colleges and categories, moderation queue
for flagged/reported listings and users, escalation workflow for
safety reports, marketplace statistics.

================================================================
9. TRUST & SAFETY (expanded — MVP-critical, not a future feature)
================================================================

- College email verification — MVP, not deferred.
- Designated safe meetup points on campus (e.g., library entrance,
  main gate/security post) suggested by default for every
  transaction — MVP.
- Block/report user and listing — MVP.
- No public display of exact hostel room or address by default (kept
  from v1 — this one was right).
- Escalation path for serious incidents: a defined owner (even if it's
  just the founder in Phase 1) and a response SLA, not just an in-app
  report button.
- Ratings/reviews can wait — for a single-campus pilot, a
  verified-identity badge (real name + college) does more for trust
  than a review system with too few reviews to be meaningful yet.

================================================================
10. DATA PRIVACY & LEGAL (new)
================================================================

- Collecting hostel/PG location and contact info triggers obligations
  under India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act (DPDP), 2023 —
  consent, data minimization, and a way for users to request deletion.
  Get this reviewed before storing precise location data, not after.
- Terms of Service should state clearly that CampusCart is a listing/
  discovery service, not a party to the transaction, and isn't liable
  for item condition or payment disputes — while still committing to a
  duty of care on moderation and safety reporting.
- Content moderation policy for prohibited/restricted items needs to
  exist before public launch, not be improvised after the first bad
  listing.

================================================================
11. TECHNICAL CONSIDERATIONS (new)
================================================================

- Platform: mobile-first responsive web app for the MVP. A
  single-campus pilot doesn't need app-store distribution friction;
  revisit native app once validated across multiple colleges (v1
  buried "mobile application" in Future Features while assuming
  phone-first usage throughout — worth resolving explicitly).
- Suggested stack (comparable student-built projects in this space
  commonly use this pattern): React/Next.js frontend, a
  Postgres-backed service (e.g. Supabase) for auth/data/storage, image
  storage with basic compression, OTP-based college email
  verification.
- Non-functional basics: image upload limits, a moderation queue with
  reasonable turnaround, and analytics wired up from day one so
  Section 4's KPIs are actually measurable — not retrofitted later.

13. MONETIZATION STRATEGY (phased, not a wish list)
================================================================

- Phase 1 (BMSIT pilot): free, no monetization. Priority is proving
  liquidity and trust with a price-sensitive, free-alternative-rich
  audience — charging anything now would push users back to
  WhatsApp/OLX.
- Phase 2 (multi-college): optional, opt-in "featured listing" boost
  for a small one-time fee. Never gate core browsing/listing behind a
  paywall.
- Phase 3+: seller subscriptions for high-volume sellers, college
  partnership/orientation-week placements, delivery commission — only
  once real demand and trust exist to justify taking a cut.

================================================================
14. GO-TO-MARKET / LAUNCH PLAN FOR BMSIT (new, concrete)
================================================================

- Timing: push seller-side signups during semester-end/hostel-vacate
  weeks (outgoing students actively want to offload items); push
  buyer-side signups during orientation week for incoming freshers.
- Seed supply before public launch: recruit 10–20 final-year students
  as early listers so the app isn't empty on day one for the first
  buyers who show up.
- Go physical, not just digital: posters/QR codes at hostel notice
  boards, canteen, and the admin block — trust and adoption on a
  single physical campus still run through in-person visibility, not
  just app-store presence.
- Gate for Phase 2: don't expand to other Bangalore colleges until the
  Section 4 KPIs are actually hit at BMSIT.

================================================================
15. ROADMAP
================================================================

- Phase 1 — BMSIT: validate core loop and safety model with real
  students; hit Section 4 KPIs.
- Phase 2 — Bangalore: expand to nearby colleges once Phase 1
  liquidity and safety record are proven.
- Phase 3 — Major Indian cities: Delhi NCR, Pune, Hyderabad, Chennai,
  Mumbai — only after a repeatable per-college launch playbook exists.
- Phase 4 — Pan-India.

17. OPEN QUESTIONS (new — needs answers before more build time)
================================================================

- What specifically makes CampusCart better than CampusKart or
  Campurchase, which already claim to operate in this exact space in
  India?
- Is BMSIT's ~6,000 students enough to reach Phase 1 liquidity targets
  alone, or should Phase 1 include 2–3 nearby colleges from the start?
- Web app or native app for the MVP — and what changes that decision
  later?
- On a small founding team, who owns safety escalation and dispute
  handling in practice?

================================================================
18. FUTURE FEATURES (kept from v1, grouped)
================================================================

- Trust: student verification, ratings & reviews
- Commerce: online payments, delivery integration, rent-instead-of-buy,
  auctions
- Discovery: AI-powered recommendations, price suggestions by
  condition, wishlist, notifications
- Growth: referral system, campus ambassadors program, inter-college
  marketplace, native mobile app
- Communication: in-app chat between buyer and seller

================================================================
19. VISION
================================================================

CampusCart aims to build a student-to-student circular marketplace
where products move from one student to the next instead of being
discarded or lost to unorganized channels — starting from BMSIT
Yelahanka, with the long-term goal of any student in India being able
to find affordable second-hand essentials from other students on their
own campus.
